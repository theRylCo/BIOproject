Biodiversity levels and scales

Species, genes, ecosystems, and function

Biodiversity includes variation at species, genetic, ecosystem, and functional levels. Richness, evenness, adaptive variation, ecological interactions, and ecosystem processes all shape stability and resilience.

Species diversity • phylogenetic diversity • genetic diversity • food webs

Species diversity

Species diversity combines richness and evenness. A community with many species that are more evenly distributed is generally more diverse than one dominated by only a few.

Environmental ethics

How should conservation decisions be judged?

Conservation decisions involve scientific evidence and ethical reasoning. Different frameworks weigh human benefit, ecological integrity, non-human value, and long-term system stability in different ways.

Intrinsic value • use value • utilitarianism • preservation • land ethic

Scenario: mountain valley dam replacement

A major reservoir supplies water and electricity to nearby communities, but aging infrastructure forces a choice: rebuild and intensify the system, partially restore the valley while maintaining reduced water storage, or fully remove the dam and prioritize ecosystem recovery.

Framework reading

A utilitarian lens weighs aggregate benefits and harms across stakeholders.

Decision result

Under this framework, a partial restoration path may balance human needs with ecological repair.
